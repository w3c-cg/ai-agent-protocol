### II. Workshop Report: E-Commerce for Humans and AI Agents

Song introduced the workshop and shared the presentation *W3C Workshop on E-Commerce for Humans and AI Agents — Study for W3C Agent Protocol Community Group*. The material describes a hybrid workshop held on September 8–9, 2026 in Zurich, jointly organized by W3C and GS1 and hosted by Google.

The following summarizes the shared material, rather than reconstructing individual remarks or CG decisions:

- **From browsing to task execution.** Agent-mediated commerce shifts the focus from human-readable pages to information and services that agents can discover, interpret, and act on for users. Merchants need accurate product representation and machine-readable information without losing the openness of the Web.
- **Fragmentation and interoperability.** The presentation surveys approaches including MCP, UCP, ACP, and A2A. It highlights repeated merchant integration work, inconsistent data models, and gaps in discovering commerce endpoints as obstacles to interoperability.
- **Security and trust.** Delegated transactions raise questions about authorization scope, sensitive data, and accountability. The material identifies risk-based permission levels, verifiable credentials, auditability, and agent and merchant identity verification as areas to explore.
- **Relevance to the CG.** E-commerce offers concrete requirements for service discovery, structured data exchange, and secure transaction flows. These use cases could help validate general agent-protocol designs and expose gaps in authorization and interoperability.
- **Possible follow-up directions.** The presentation proposes an e-commerce vertical task force, interoperability guidelines for existing commerce-agent protocols, and extensions to security and authorization models for transactional use cases. These are proposals in the shared material, not recorded commitments by the CG.
